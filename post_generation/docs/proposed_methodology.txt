Problem Statement & Research GapCurrent post-generation manipulation techniques for evading AI text detection suffer from a critical limitation regarding cross-detector robustness. This proposal addresses the following structured gap in the current literature:* The Status Quo: State-of-the-art evaders (e.g., GradEscape, HMGC) successfully bypass specific detectors using gradient-based continuous relaxation or mask-based synonym replacement.* The Limitation: These methods optimize naturalness using localized semantic constraints (e.g., USE, POS consistency) and limited statistical signals (primarily perplexity). They completely lack explicit modeling of macroscopic, document-level stylometric distributions, such as burstiness, lexical entropy, and rhetorical flow.* The Consequence (Adversarial Overfitting): Because these evaders optimize heavily against a single detector architecture and ignore document-level naturalness, they overfit. They fail to transfer across heterogeneous detector paradigms (e.g., text optimized to fool a neural classifier will often fail against a statistical zero-shot detector).* The Proposed Novelty: This project introduces a joint-optimization framework that simultaneously targets neural misclassification and macroscopic stylometric naturalness, aiming to achieve Cross-Paradigm Transferable Adversarial Paraphrasing.Methodology1. Experimental Framework and Threat ModelThe experiments operate strictly within a post-generation manipulation framework, where the evader only receives the final autoregressive output of a target LLM. We define the adversary's capabilities under a Gray-Box Transfer Threat Model:* Attacker Knowledge: The adversary has white-box access to a surrogate open-source neural detector (e.g., RoBERTa). The attacker uses this surrogate to compute gradients and optimize the adversarial paraphrasing.* Attacker Constraint: The adversary has absolute zero-knowledge (black-box) of the actual target detectors deployed in the wild, which may feature entirely different architectures or statistical paradigms. The adversary must craft text on the surrogate that is robust enough to transfer blindly to these unknown systems.2. Proposed Evader Pipeline: Stylometry-Aware Differentiable ParaphraserBuilding upon the continuous relaxation and pseudo-embedding techniques from Meng et al. (2025), we will re-engineer the optimization objective to enforce document-level naturalness.The engine iteratively updates text parameters by minimizing a novel joint loss function:\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{tota}\ell}=\alpha\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{adv}}+\beta\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{sem}}+\gamma\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{sty}\ell\mathcal{e}} * \mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{adv}} (Adversarial Loss): Backpropagated from the surrogate neural detector to force misclassification.* \mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{sem}} (Semantic & Syntactic Loss): Ensures token-level and sentence-level meaning is preserved, utilizing constraints similar to the USE and POS filtering seen in Zhou et al. (2024).* \mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{sty}\ell\mathcal{e}} (Novel Stylometric Loss): This loss term penalizes deviations from human-baseline document properties. It actively minimizes the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the manipulated text's sentence length variance (burstiness) and the distribution found in a genuine human corpus.3. Datasets and Target Detectors* Data: A curated corpus of human-written text paired with AI-generated counterparts (e.g., LLaMA-3, GPT-5).* Evaluation Targets (The "Unknown" Black-Boxes):o Neural Classifiers: DeBERTa models fine-tuned on diverse AI vs. Human datasets.o Statistical/Zero-Shot Detectors: Methods relying on probability curvature, such as DetectGPT (Mitchell et al., 2023).Evaluation1. Baseline Results and Target MetricsBaseline evaluation will establish the unmanipulated AI text's detection rate. To measure the success of the joint optimization, we will track:* Surrogate Attack Success Rate (ASR): The evasion rate on the white-box surrogate model used during optimization.* Cross-Paradigm Transferability Rate (CPTR): The core metric. This measures the evasion success rate when the text, optimized purely on the neural surrogate, is blindly evaluated against the black-box statistical detectors (e.g., DetectGPT) and unseen neural classifiers.* Semantic Preservation: BLEU and BERTScore to validate local naturalness.* Macroscopic Stylometry: Quantifying the actual shift in burstiness and lexical entropy to verify the \mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{sty}\ell\mathcal{e}} constraint functioned as intended.2. AnalysisWe will conduct an ablation study comparing our "Stylometry-Aware" evader against baseline implementations of GradEscape (pure gradient-based, \mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{adv}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{sem}}) and HMGC (mask-based synonym replacement).The analysis will test the hypothesis that while baseline evaders achieve near-perfect ASR on the surrogate model, their CPTR degrades severely against statistical zero-shot models. Conversely, the proposed evader—by explicitly constraining gradient updates to mimic macroscopic human distributions—is expected to maintain a highly stable CPTR across all heterogeneous detectors. We will analyze which specific stylometric features (variance in syntactic tree depth vs. sentence length) contribute most to this cross-detector robustness.References1. Meng, Z., et al. (2025).
